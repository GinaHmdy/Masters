ASTD-PD, explained with its own numbers

Adaptive Spectral-Topological Debiasing with Pareto Dual-Stream Fusion: a two-stream LightGCN that tries to recommend less popular items. Every number on this page is read from the trained models and result files in this repository by scripts/build_explainer.py; nothing is typed by hand. Pick a dataset at the top and every section follows it.

1. The problem: LightGCN concentrates on popular items

LightGCN smooths every embedding toward its graph neighbours. An item with many interactions sits in many users' neighbourhoods, so its embedding ends up close to most users and it wins the dot-product ranking for most of them. The chart shows where LightGCN's top-20 slots go, split by item-popularity decile (decile 0 = the 10% least-interacted items, decile 9 = the 10% most-interacted).

3. Training: where the popularity reduction is learned

Both streams are trained together with a sampled-softmax loss. For each (user, positive item) the model draws one negative item and pushes the positive's score above it. The negatives are what the model learns to rank below the positive, so the way they are drawn shapes what the model learns.

L = λ_final·L_fused + λ_acc·L_A + λ_deb·mean((1+t_pos)·L_D) + λ_ind·L_ind + decay·L2 L_x = −log softmax([pos_x, neg_x − log q(neg)])[0] (log-Q corrected sampled softmax)

src/astdpd/model.py:264-305 · src/astdpd/sampler.py:43-74

The popularity-stratified negative sampler

Each negative is drawn by one of three rules: uniform over all items, matched (a random item from the same popularity decile as the positive), or hard (a random item from the top decile). Since positives are mostly popular items, matched and hard negatives are mostly popular too. The model is then repeatedly told "this popular item is not what the user picked", which stops popularity alone from earning a high score. Log-Q subtracts the log of each negative's sampling probability so that this over-sampling does not simply bias the scores the other way.
