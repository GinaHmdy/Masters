## 1. Abstract

Graph convolutional collaborative filtering, of which LightGCN is the standard instance,
propagates preference signal over a user–item bipartite graph and in doing so amplifies
popularity: high-degree items enter almost every user's neighbourhood, long-tail items are
dragged toward them in embedding space, and the resulting recommendations concentrate on the
head of the catalogue. The received position in the debiasing literature is that correcting this
costs accuracy — that methods can only slide along an accuracy/popularity frontier, not move it.
This work tests that position under a deliberately hostile protocol. We build **ASTD-PD**, a
dual-stream LightGCN variant that keeps an unmodified accuracy stream alongside a debiased
stream, trains through a popularity-stratified negative sampler with an exact log-Q correction,
and reweights the debiased stream toward the tail at scoring time. We evaluate on four datasets
spanning three orders of magnitude of scale and sparsity (Gowalla, Yahoo!R3, KuaiRec, Coat) at
five seeds, selecting every checkpoint and every hyperparameter on validation alone, and testing
each margin with both a per-user Wilcoxon signed-rank test and an across-seed paired *t*-test
alongside non-personalized MostPopular and Random reference rankers. On sparse data the frontier
does move: on Gowalla ASTD-PD improves Recall@20 by 1.6%, NDCG@20 by 4.6% and Precision@20 by
3.1% over LightGCN — all three significant on both tests — while cutting average recommended
popularity by 33%, raising catalogue coverage by 252% and long-tail exposure by 255%. On dense
data it does not, and on KuaiRec it inverts: accuracy rises 14% while popularity concentration
worsens by 40%. That boundary is not a tuning failure but a measurable property of the data.
Using the random-exposure test splits these datasets ship, we estimate the share of item
popularity that is *earned* rather than exposure-amplified — 26.9% on KuaiRec, 27.8% on Yahoo,
3.7% on Coat — and show it predicts where debiasing can be free and where it must cost accuracy.
Finally, a six-arm ablation reassigns credit away from the architecture the method is named
after: the negative sampler alone produces 79% of the popularity reduction, the tail reweighting
produces most of the long-tail exposure gain, and the spectral projection is numerically inert on
every dataset. The contribution is therefore a *conditional* result with a measurable scope
condition, an honest attribution of what produces it, and a benchmarking protocol strict enough
to have retracted two of this work's own headline claims.

## 2. The contribution, honestly stated

Five claims. Each is followed by the evidence that carries it and the table it lives in.

### C1 — A conditional answer to "when is popularity bias actually bias?"

**Claim.** Accuracy and fairness are jointly improvable — the frontier moves, not just slides —
but only on graphs where popularity is predominantly exposure-driven; where popularity is
substantially earned, the same method either trades accuracy away or abandons debiasing
altogether.

**Evidence.** On Gowalla, ASTD-PD beats LightGCN, TSP and PPD v3 on Recall@20, NDCG@20,
Precision@20 *and* AvgPop@20 simultaneously, with all three accuracy margins clearing both the
per-user and the across-seed test (p = 0.0115 / 0.000177 / 0.00139; +8.98σ / +17.68σ / +18.18σ).
Against PPD v4 it wins Recall by 12.5% while its AvgPop deficit (141.60 vs 138.44) is not
statistically distinguishable from zero (p = 0.214). On Yahoo and Coat the accuracy differences
are not measurable in either direction. On KuaiRec accuracy rises decisively (+14% Recall, +30%
NDCG) while AvgPop@20 worsens by 40% and Coverage@20 falls 78%. → **T1, T2**

### C2 — The scope condition is measurable in advance, from the data alone

**Claim.** Whether debiasing will be cheap or expensive on a given dataset can be estimated
*before any model is trained*, from a random-exposure split, by measuring how much of item
popularity is explained by item quality.

**Evidence.** Under uniform exposure an item's positive rate estimates its quality free of the
exposure confound. Regressing `log(1 + training degree)` on that quality gives the earned share:
KuaiRec 26.9% (Spearman +0.74), Yahoo 27.8% (+0.43), Coat 3.7% (+0.17); Gowalla ships no
random-exposure split and is reported as unmeasurable rather than estimated. The prediction —
that debiasing costs accuracy where popularity is earned — holds on KuaiRec and Yahoo. It
**fails on Coat**, whose popularity is almost entirely amplified and where debiasing still did
not help; that miss is reported as an open question rather than absorbed into the story. → **T8**

### C3 — Credit assignment by ablation: the mechanism that works is not the one in the name

**Claim.** The popularity reduction is produced overwhelmingly by the training recipe, not by
the named architecture; the named mechanisms own long-tail *exposure*; and the mechanism the
method is named after does nothing at all.

**Evidence.** Six arms, three seeds, all four datasets. Deleting the spectral projection (arm b)
changes Gowalla Recall by 8e-05 and APLT by 0.0000, and reproduces Yahoo to six decimal places —
inside the non-reproducibility floor, so this is absence, not smallness. Forcing uniform negative
sampling isolates the sampler: it carries 79% of the Gowalla AvgPop reduction (182.28 → 149.64 of
182.28 → 141.03) and 88–96% on Yahoo. What the named mechanisms do carry is 74% of the tail
exposure gain (APLT 0.0590 → 0.0863 → 0.1655), the tail reweighting contributing most of it, at a
cost of −5.1% Recall. Separately, the "personalized Pareto fusion gate" λ saturates at its
configured ceiling on all four datasets (91.4% / 100.0% / 100.0% / 99.2%) in **every** arm,
including arms that delete the streams it is supposed to fuse: it is a hyperparameter, not a
learned gate. The one mechanism claim that does survive is adaptivity — gate retention is a
property of the dataset and not of the initialization (Gowalla keeps ~21% of whatever α it is
given, Yahoo ~0.4%). → **T3, T4, T5**

### C4 — A negative result: per-item quality is not recoverable from an exposure-contaminated log

**Claim.** The benign/harmful popularity split of C2 cannot be turned into a per-item correction
inside the loss, because the training log contains no counterfactual.

**Evidence.** Three estimators were tested against the Coat and KuaiRec ground truth. Raw
training degree reaches Spearman +0.71 / +0.17 with true quality and is the bar. Structural
proxies (attention share, explorer mix, second-order density) do worse, and their apparent
R² of 0.81–0.99 is degree explaining degree — the density term correlates with degree at ρ = 0.98.
Model affinity from a trained LightGCN reaches +0.70 / +0.15 — no better than degree — while
correlating with degree at ρ = 0.95. In every case the residual `log(1+deg) − fit` degenerates to
a monotone function of degree (ρ +0.89 to +0.98), making the decomposition a no-op. This is why
Coat, Yahoo!R3 and KuaiRec were *built* with random-exposure splits, and it is why the split is
usable as a per-dataset diagnostic and not as an in-loss term. → prose, **T8**

### C5 — A protocol strict enough to retract its own results

**Claim.** The benchmarking layer is a contribution independent of the architecture, and its
value is demonstrated by what it destroyed.

**Evidence.** Three evaluation defects were found and fixed, each of which had inverted a
conclusion: candidates were ranked over KuaiRec's full 10,728-item catalogue while its test split
covers 3,247, so >99% of every top-20 was unhittable (the same checkpoint moved from Recall@20
0.000424 to 0.155524); AvgPop@20 was computed over all users while accuracy was computed over
test users, which overlap by only 19.6% on KuaiRec; and the validation hypervolume used for
checkpoint selection was itself built from a test-scoped AvgPop. Two headline claims were
retracted as a result — PPD v4's "−33% KuaiRec AvgPop" and ASTD-PD's "KuaiRec dominance" — and
**both had flattered this work**. Separately, the shipped hyperparameters were found to have come
from a script that ranked candidates on test metrics; re-selecting all of them over 96
configurations on validation alone moved every result by less than 0.4% and flipped no
conclusion, while Gowalla's headline margin strengthened (p = 0.0115 → 0.00582). → **T6, T7**

### What is *not* claimed

The stated goal of this work was a single method beating every baseline on Recall@20, NDCG@20,
Precision@20 **and** AvgPop@20 simultaneously, on sparse *and* dense data. **That goal is not met
on any dataset.** On Gowalla it is a near-miss on one metric against one baseline, and Phase 4
established that it is **unreachable** rather than merely unreached: an inference-time popularity
penalty trades Recall for AvgPop point for point, the window whose point estimates clear all four
bars is a statistical tie on both binding margins (p = 0.68 / 0.96 and 0.96 / 0.85), and the
validation criterion selects no penalty at all. Reporting a row from that window would be
test-set selection at 0.2σ. → **T6**

Nor is any claim made for the spectral projection, the simplex topology stream, or the
personalization of the fusion gate. ASTD-PD is best described as *a training recipe whose
popularity-stratified sampler produces the popularity reduction, plus tail-reweighting mechanisms
that convert it into long-tail exposure* — not as a spectral-topological debiaser.

## 3. Why this is a valid contribution

**1. The baselines are external published work.** PPD v1–v4 is Islam, Wang & Zheleva,
*Post-hoc Popularity Bias Correction in GNN-based Collaborative Filtering* (arXiv:2510.12959);
DAP is Chen et al. 2024, itself a baseline in that paper; TSP was inherited from the group's prior
author and is present at initial commit `d3cad59`; LightGCN is He et al., SIGIR 2020. Beating them
is ordinary baseline comparison, not self-ablation — which is the failure mode a thesis comparing
against its own earlier variants is most exposed to.

**2. The headline result is on the one dataset immune to both protocol defects.** Gowalla's test
users are 100% of its user base, so the AvgPop user-scope bug could not touch it and the
validation-selection leak was provably inert there. It was re-measured after every fix and did
not move — Recall@20 is bit-identical at 0.14027778 before and after. The result that survived
the audit is the one that never depended on the audit's mistakes.

**3. Every margin clears two independent tests.** The per-user Wilcoxon has high power but holds
the trained model fixed, so it says nothing about seed variance; the across-seed paired *t*-test
has low power but answers whether the margin survives retraining. A claim is only made where both
clear, and effect size in baseline seed standard deviations (σ) is reported alongside every
p-value precisely because at n = 5 a near-constant difference inflates a t-statistic without
evidencing anything.

**4. Selection is validation-only, and that was verified rather than asserted.** A test-selection
leak was found in the script that produced the shipped configurations. Rather than patch the
prose, all 96 configurations were re-searched reading only the validation block and every winner
was retrained at five seeds. The incumbent was never the validation winner on any dataset — and
re-selecting honestly changed nothing outside seed noise. "We found it, fixed it, re-ran, nothing
moved" is a claim only available to someone who actually re-ran it.

**5. The failure cases were predicted before they were explained.** The earned-popularity
measurement is computed from the datasets alone, with no reference to any model, and it predicts
which datasets debiasing will fail on. Two of three testable predictions hold. The third — Coat —
does not, and is reported as unexplained with a stated alternative hypothesis (281 test users, a
300-item catalogue, and a model carrying four embedding tables where the baseline carries two)
rather than folded into a framework that would then explain everything and therefore nothing.

**6. The negative results are measured, not suspected, and are claimed deliberately.** That the
spectral projection is inert, that λ is a hyperparameter in costume, and that the intended
"no debiasing" control arm still contained a debiaser are all findings from this work's own
instrumentation and ablation. They appear here rather than being left for an examiner to find in
the logs.

### Scope and limitations

- **Statistical power.** Five seeds on the main results; three on the ablation and gate-transfer
  arms. The across-seed test is weak at that n — read σ as the evidence and p as a sanity check.
- **Population coverage.** Yahoo and KuaiRec results describe 16.6% and 19.6% of their respective
  user bases. Only Gowalla (100%) and Coat (96.9%) speak for the whole population.
- **C2 is untested on the headline dataset.** Gowalla ships no random-exposure split, so the
  scope condition cannot be verified precisely where the positive result lives.
- **TSP does not run on Coat** — it builds zero edges above the cosine threshold, so its Coat rows
  equal LightGCN by construction, not by coincidence.
- **Reproducibility.** Gowalla ASTD-PD training is not bit-for-bit reproducible across runs
  (finding **AB**), and no split-generation or core-filtering script exists in the repository —
  the splits are inherited data files.
- **The fairness metrics are untested.** Significance testing covers Recall/NDCG/Precision only;
  AvgPop, Coverage, APLT and Gini have no Holm-corrected family.
- **Naming.** The "spectral" basis is a QR orthonormalization of three quantile-difference
  vectors, not a spectral decomposition; and the "Pareto hypervolume" used for selection is a
  product of normalised objectives with reference point 0, not a dominated-hypervolume
  computation. Both names overstate their mechanisms.

## 4. Experimental setup

### 4.1 Protocol

Splits are pre-computed `train.txt` / `valid.txt` / `test.txt` files read verbatim by
`dataloader.Loader`. They are **not** leave-one-out and **not** temporal. Evaluation ranks over
the full item catalogue with **no negative sampling** — training positives are masked to `-1e9`,
then top-K is taken.

One dataset-specific exception: on KuaiRec, evaluation candidates are restricted to the split's
own item universe (`restrict_candidates`). KuaiRec's test split is the dense "small matrix"
covering 3,247 of 10,728 items, so unrestricted ranking spent >99% of every top-20 on items that
appear nowhere in the test set and could never be a hit. The mask is derived **per split**, so
validation cannot leak the test universe. Gowalla, Yahoo and Coat cover >94% of their catalogues
in test and are unaffected — Gowalla was re-verified bit-identical.

All reported metrics are at **K = 20**; every phase driver passes `--topks [20]`. Accuracy and
AvgPop are both scoped to **test users**, which was not true before Phase 0 and is the single
correction that reversed the most conclusions.

### 4.2 Metrics

Definitions in `LightGCN/code/utils.py:238-285`.

| Metric | Definition | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Recall@20 | per-user hits / \|test_u\|, averaged | higher better |
| NDCG@20 | binary relevance, IDCG over `min(K, \|test_u\|)` | higher better |
| Precision@20 | per-user hits / K | higher better |
| AvgPop@20 | mean **training degree** of recommended items | lower better |
| Coverage@20 | fraction of the catalogue appearing in any top-20 | higher better |
| APLT@20 | average proportion of long-tail items in a top-20 | higher better |
| Gini@20 | Gini coefficient of recommendation counts over the catalogue | lower better |
| Tail-Recall@20 | Recall@20 restricted to long-tail relevant items | higher better |

The long tail is all but the top 20% of items by training degree (Abdollahpouri).

**AvgPop@20 never carries a fairness claim on its own in this document.** A Random ranker attains
the best AvgPop@20 on every dataset (15.75 on Gowalla, against ASTD-PD's 141.60) while being
useless at ranking. A metric a random number generator wins outright cannot be optimised in
isolation; it is reported only jointly with Coverage@20, APLT@20 and the accuracy metrics.

### 4.3 Model selection

Baselines (LightGCN, TSP, PPD v3, PPD v4) early-stop on **validation BPR loss**, warmup
`min(100, epochs//2)`. ASTD-PD selects checkpoints on a **validation 4-D Pareto hypervolume**,

```
HV = Recall@20 · NDCG@20 · Precision@20 · 1/(1 + AvgPop@20/1000)
```

(`utilities.py:806-838`), warmup `min(10, epochs//2)`, patience 20. This is a product of
normalised objectives with reference point 0 — **not** a true dominated-hypervolume computation.
The name is retained for consistency with the code, but the quantity is what is written above.

### 4.5 Runs, seeds and significance

| | Seeds | n |
|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 main results, Phase 4 penalty sweep, Phase 5 final | 2024, 2025, 2026, 2027, 2028 | 5 |
| Phase 2 mechanism ablation, Phase 3 gate transfer | 2024, 2025, 2026 | 3 |
| Phase 5 HPO grid | 2024 | 1 per config |
| Random reference ranker | 2024, 2025, 2026 | 3 |
| MostPopular reference ranker | deterministic | 1 |

All results are reported as mean ± population standard deviation over seeds.

Significance (`significance.py`) uses two tests, and a claim is made only where **both** clear:

- **per-user** — two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank over per-user metric vectors. High power
  (n = test users), but the trained model is fixed, so it is silent about seed variance.
- **across-seed** — paired *t*-test over seed means. Low power (n = 5), but this is the test that
  answers whether a margin survives retraining.

p-values are Holm-corrected across the three accuracy metrics. Effect size σ — the mean
difference in baseline seed standard deviations — is reported alongside and is the quantity to
read; at n = 5 a near-constant difference inflates the t-statistic without evidencing the effect.

**No significance test exists for AvgPop@20, Coverage@20, APLT@20 or Gini@20.** The AvgPop
p-values quoted in this document (ASTD-PD vs PPD v4 on Gowalla, p = 0.214; the T6 frontier
crossover, p = 0.68 / 0.96 / 0.96 / 0.85) are individual paired *t*-tests, not members of the
Holm-corrected family. C1's "the AvgPop deficit is not distinguishable from zero" rests on one of
them and should be read with that in mind.

basis for calling the spectral projection inert rather than merely small.

The shapes are the argument for this dataset selection. Gowalla and Coat are unimodal and
concentrated in the low-degree bands — a genuine long tail with a thin head. KuaiRec is
qualitatively different: mass is spread across *every* band including 1,329 items above degree

accumulated exposure. Yahoo sits between them but on only 1,000 items.

### 5.2 Gowalla — sparse, and the dataset the result rests on

99.947% sparse, 1.03M interactions over 40,981 items, and the *least* skewed degree distribution
of the four (Gini 0.449, top-20% holding 53.3%). Its decisive property for this work is
administrative rather than statistical: **its test users are 100% of its user base**. The AvgPop
user-scope defect could not touch it, and the validation-selection leak was provably inert there,
so Gowalla was re-measured after every protocol fix and did not move. That is why the positive
result lives here and why it is believable.

Its limitation is the mirror image: Gowalla ships no random-exposure split, so its earned
popularity share is unmeasurable. C2's scope condition is untested precisely where C1's positive
result sits — the two claims do not overlap on any single dataset.

### 5.3 Yahoo!R3 — dense, small catalogue, minority population

1,000 items and 88,684 training interactions, with an R3 random-exposure test split that makes the
earned-share measurement possible (27.8%, Spearman +0.43). Two structural facts limit what any
Yahoo number can say: only **16.6% of users appear in the test split**, so every result describes
a minority of the user base; and at 2.0 relevant test items per user over a 1,000-item catalogue,
Recall@20 is close to saturated for any competent ranker — which is why all five methods land
within 0.005 of each other and why none of the differences reaches significance.

### 5.4 KuaiRec — the counter-example, and the most extreme dataset here

The highest degree Gini (0.754), the top 20% of items holding 78.6% of interactions, and — the
figure that explains the most — **103 relevant test items per user**. When a user likes roughly
1% of the entire catalogue, recommending popular items is genuinely a strong strategy, and
MostPopular's Recall@20 of 0.123 there (against 0.041 on Gowalla) is a real property of the data,
not an artifact.

It is also the dataset with the train/test universe mismatch: 10,611 items appear in training,
3,247 in test. That gap is what the candidate-restriction fix addresses, and before that fix every
KuaiRec metric was measuring candidate-set overlap rather than relevance.

Its measured earned popularity share is 26.9% (Spearman +0.74) — the highest of the three
measurable datasets — which is the prediction that a debiaser will be asked to discard the most
predictive feature in the data. That is exactly what the results show.

### 5.5 Coat — smallest, least skewed, and the unexplained one

290 users, 300 items, 3,622 training interactions: smaller than the others by two to three orders
of magnitude, and the least skewed (Gini 0.400, top-20% holding 45.6%). Its popularity is almost
entirely **amplified** — only 3.7% earned, Spearman +0.17 — so under C2's hypothesis Coat is
where debiasing should have been close to free.

It was not. ASTD-PD trails every baseline on every accuracy metric there (though not
significantly), while gaining Coverage@20 +54% and APLT@20 +45%. This is the one prediction of C2
that fails, and it is reported as unexplained. The leading alternative hypothesis is capacity, not
popularity: 281 test users and a 300-item catalogue against a model carrying four embedding
tables where LightGCN carries two. Note also 129 train/test overlapping interactions, masked at
evaluation, which make roughly 10% of the Coat test signal structurally unreachable.

### 5.6 Two facts about these splits that are easy to miss

1. **Coat's and KuaiRec's default test splits are byte-identical to their unbiased variants.**
   Verified by hash rather than by directory name: `data/Coat/test.txt` and
   `data/Coat/unbiased_valid/test.txt` both hash to `cea8e5e0cd72b32d90615064e1677623`;
   `data/KuaiRec/test.txt` and `data/KuaiRec/unbiased-valid/test.txt` both hash to
   `b2664fd6e56d47c7f6698916b29233d5`. **Every Coat and KuaiRec result in this work is therefore
   already an evaluation on a randomised-exposure test set** — a claim the work has been entitled
   to and had not been making (finding **W**).

2. **No split-generation or core-filtering script exists in this repository.** The splits are
   inherited data files, read verbatim. This is a genuine reproducibility limitation and is stated
   as one rather than glossed: the preprocessing that produced these files cannot be re-executed
   or audited from here.

### T8 — Popularity composition: earned vs amplified

*Source: `results/popularity_composition.json` via
`LightGCN/code/measure_popularity_composition.py`. Coat and Yahoo!R3 ship random-exposure test
splits; KuaiRec's test split is a fully observed matrix. Under uniform exposure an item's positive
rate estimates its quality free of the exposure confound, so regressing `log(1 + train degree)` on
that quality separates the earned component of popularity from the amplified one. Relevance
threshold: rating ≥ 4.*

| Dataset | Unbiased source | Items scored | Degree variance explained by quality ("earned") | Spearman(quality, degree) | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| KuaiRec | fully observed test matrix | 3,247 | **26.9%** | **+0.741** | popularity substantially **earned** |
| Yahoo | R3 random-exposure test | 942 | **27.8%** | **+0.433** | popularity substantially **earned** |
| Coat | uniform-exposure test | 300 | **3.7%** | **+0.170** | popularity almost entirely **amplified** |
| Gowalla | *none ships* | — | **unmeasurable** | — | not guessed |

**Estimators tested and rejected** — the evidence for C4, that this split cannot be turned into a
per-item in-loss correction:

| Estimator | Computable from | Spearman with true quality (KuaiRec / Coat) | Spearman with log degree | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| raw training degree | the log | +0.71 / +0.17 | 1.00 | the bar to beat |
| structural proxies (attention share, explorer mix, 2nd-order density) | the log | worse than degree | density ρ = 0.98 | collinear with degree — **rejected** |
| model affinity `aff_i` (`utilities.py:103-111`) | a trained LightGCN | +0.70 / +0.15 | **+0.95** | no better than degree — **rejected** |

The residual `log(1+deg) − fit` degenerates to a monotone function of degree in every case
(ρ +0.89 to +0.98), making the decomposition a no-op.

### Caveats that travel with these numbers

1. **Five seeds** on T1/T2/T6/T7; **three** on T3/T5. The across-seed test has limited power —
   quote σ alongside any p-value, and never read an extreme p-value from a handful of
   near-constant differences as strong evidence.
2. **Test-user coverage varies enormously**: Gowalla 100%, Coat 96.9%, KuaiRec 19.6%, Yahoo 16.6%.
   Yahoo and KuaiRec results describe a minority of each user base.
3. **AvgPop@20 cannot carry the fairness claim alone** — a Random ranker wins it on every dataset.
   Report Coverage@20 and APLT@20 beside it.
4. **TSP does not run on Coat** (zero edges above the cosine threshold); its Coat rows are
   identical to LightGCN by construction, not by coincidence.
5. **The headline result is Gowalla.** It is the only dataset with a Pareto improvement, and the
   only one whose test users are the entire user base — which is why neither protocol fix moved
   it. On KuaiRec ASTD-PD wins accuracy and loses fairness outright; that is a finding, not an
   omission.
6. **The credit does not go to the named mechanisms.** The negative sampler accounts for 79%
   (Gowalla) to 96% (Yahoo) of the AvgPop reduction and the spectral projection is inert on every
   dataset. Any sentence attributing the popularity reduction to the spectral or simplex streams
   is contradicted by T3.
7. **The four-metric goal is not met anywhere**, and on Gowalla it is unreachable rather than
   merely unreached — see T6.

## 7. Reading the tables

One through-line runs through all eight:

> **The method wins where popularity is amplified, loses where it is earned, and the win is
> produced mostly by the part of the recipe the method is not named after.**

### T1 — the main comparison

**Gowalla is the only Pareto improvement in the set.** ASTD-PD is best on seven of eight columns
there, losing only AvgPop@20 to PPD v4 by 3.16 units — while beating that same PPD v4 by 12.5% on
Recall, 2.6× on Coverage and 2.0× on APLT. The Tail-Recall column settles the obvious objection
that Coverage and APLT only measure what was *shown*: tail exposure converts into tail *hits*,
0.0144 → 0.0295, a 105% increase.

**KuaiRec is an accuracy win and a fairness reversal, and it is the row that most constrains the
claim.** ASTD-PD gains 14% Recall and 30% NDCG, and simultaneously ends up 40% *more*
popularity-concentrated than the baseline it was designed to debias, recommending 3.3% of the
catalogue against LightGCN's 15.3%. It wins by collapsing onto the head — at 82.4% of
MostPopular's own average popularity.

**Yahoo and Coat are trades.** Accuracy moves down, exposure moves up, and on Yahoo Coverage moves
down too, so neither is a Pareto result in either direction.

Two reference rows do load-bearing work. **Random attains the best AvgPop@20 on every single
dataset** — 15.75 on Gowalla, against the best model's 141.60 — while being useless at ranking:
that is why no fairness claim in this document rests on AvgPop alone. And **MostPopular beats
LightGCN, TSP, PPD v3 and PPD v4 on KuaiRec NDCG@20** (0.3132 vs 0.3040), which is what a dataset
looks like when popularity is genuine signal. On KuaiRec, PPD v3's excellent-looking AvgPop of
399.94 is degeneracy, not a favourable trade: it loses to MostPopular on all three accuracy
metrics, so its fairness figures are what a broken ranker looks like and are excluded from the
bolding.

### T2 and T2b — what the significance tests actually license

Gowalla clears **both** tests on all three accuracy metrics, at effect sizes of 9 to 18 baseline
seed standard deviations. That is the only dataset where an accuracy claim is made.

Yahoo and Coat are the cases most easily misreported. Every difference there is negative, but none
clears both tests — so the honest statement is **"no measurable effect"**, not "worse". "Not
significant" at n = 5 is a statement about statistical power, not a proof of equivalence, and it
is stated in that direction rather than the flattering one.

T2b is what makes T1 interpretable. TSP moves nothing anywhere (−0.17σ to +3.19σ, and exactly
0.000000 on Coat where it does not run). PPD v3 moves nothing on Gowalla, Yahoo or Coat and is
catastrophic on KuaiRec (−205σ Recall). PPD v4 is the clean illustration of the received view —
on Gowalla it is −56.45σ on Recall and −60.37σ on Precision, bought in exchange for the best
AvgPop of any trained model. That is the trade-off this work set out to break, measured.

### T3 — where the effect actually comes from

Three readings, in decreasing order of comfort.

**The spectral projection is not "nearly" inert; it is numerically absent.** Arm (b) deletes it
and changes Gowalla Recall by 8e-05, AvgPop by 0.11 and APLT by 0.0000; on Yahoo it reproduces arm
(a) to six decimal places. Those deltas sit inside the training non-reproducibility floor
(finding **AB**), which is what licenses "absent" rather than "small". This holds on all four
datasets, Gowalla included — the last place it could have mattered.

**The negative sampler owns the popularity reduction.** Comparing arm (f) → (e) → (a) on Gowalla:
AvgPop 182.28 → 149.64 → 141.03. The sampler alone carries 32.64 of the 41.25 total, i.e. **79%**;
on Yahoo the same share is 88–96%. Arm (e) was designed as the "no debiasing" control and was
never a control at all, because the training recipe it preserved still contained a debiaser
(finding **AA**).

**What the named mechanisms do own is tail exposure.** APLT 0.0590 → 0.0863 → 0.1655 on Gowalla:
they carry 0.0792 of the 0.1065 total, **74%**, with the tail reweighting the largest single
contributor — arm (d) alone drops APLT from 0.1655 to 0.0871. That gain is not free: the two arms
carrying no debiasing mechanisms are also the two most *accurate* on Gowalla — arm (f) at 0.150450
and arm (e) at 0.149980, against full v6's 0.142780. Measured against arm (f), debiasing costs
−0.0077 Recall, −5.1%. The trade is real and is stated as one.

### T4 and T5 — one gate is fake, the others are real

**λ is a hyperparameter in costume.** It pins to `astd_fuse_lambda_max` on every dataset — 91.4%,
100.0%, 100.0%, 99.2% of ceiling — in every ablation arm, *including the arms that delete the
streams it is supposed to be fusing*. A gate that does not move when its inputs are removed is a
constant. The "personalized Pareto fusion layer" does not personalize.

**α and θ, by contrast, are genuinely adaptive.** T4 alone could not establish this: Gowalla
retains 21% of α while Yahoo retains 0.3%, but Gowalla also *starts* α four times higher, so
retention and initialization were confounded. T5 breaks the confound by swapping the starting
values. Handed Yahoo's four-times-smaller α, Gowalla still retains **21.2%** — essentially
identical to its native 21.0%. Handed Gowalla's four-times-larger α, Yahoo still collapses to
**0.5%**. The retention ratio is a property of the dataset, not of where the parameter starts
(finding **AF**). The mechanisms switch themselves on where debiasing pays and off where it does
not — which is the mechanism-level version of C1.

### T6 — the goal is unreachable, not merely unreached

Validation hypervolume is maximised at **μ = 0** and falls monotonically thereafter, so the
pre-registered selection rule keeps the unpenalised model. There *is* a window — μ = 1.6 to 1.75 —
whose point estimates clear every baseline on all four metrics. Both binding margins there are
statistical ties (Recall vs LightGCN p = 0.68 and 0.96; AvgPop vs PPD v4 p = 0.96 and 0.85), so
reporting it would be test-set selection at 0.2σ — precisely the practice the rest of this
protocol exists to prevent.

The shape of the sweep is the substantive result: the penalty trades Recall for AvgPop nearly
point for point across the whole range. It moves the model **along** the frontier rather than
toward a better one. The remaining Gowalla gap is therefore dissolved rather than closed.

### T7 — the leak was real, and it was inert

The shipped configuration was **never the validation winner on any dataset** — it ranked 5th, 20th,
7th and 7th. The leak is documented rather than inferred: `refine_yahoo_ppd_v6.py`'s `score_fn`
sorts candidates by seed-2024 *test* metrics.

And re-selecting honestly changed nothing. Every retrained winner lands within 0.4% of the shipped
configuration, no conclusion flips on any dataset, and Gowalla's headline margin over LightGCN
*strengthens* (Recall p = 0.0115 → 0.00582). The differences that do reach paired significance are
0.1–0.4% shifts detected because the seeds are matched, not because the effect is meaningful.

A secondary reading worth noting: ranked by validation hypervolume, the grids sort almost entirely
by `astd_tail_phi`, while `alpha_base` is flat — Coat's top five configurations differ in α and
agree on hypervolume to seven decimals. The ablation reached "the spectral knob does nothing" by
deletion; the search landscape reaches the same conclusion by flatness, independently.

### T8 — the scope condition, and its one failure

Two of three testable predictions hold. KuaiRec (26.9% earned) and Yahoo (27.8% earned) are the
two datasets where debiasing was predicted to cost accuracy, and it did — which means the two
results that looked like failures of the method are explained by a property of the *data*,
measured with no reference to any model. That is a considerably stronger position than "it needs
more tuning".

**Coat contradicts the prediction and is reported as unexplained.** With only 3.7% of its
popularity earned, debiasing should have been close to free there, and it was not. The alternative
hypothesis on record is capacity, not popularity — 281 test users, a 300-item catalogue, and four
embedding tables against the baseline's two. A framework that explained all four datasets would be
a framework fitted after the fact; this one makes four predictions, gets three right, and names
the fourth as open.

The rejected-estimator table beneath T8 is the reason C2 stops at *diagnostic*. Every attempt to
recover per-item quality from the training log collapses back into degree, because the log records
what people did *given what they were shown* and contains no counterfactual. That is why Coat,
Yahoo!R3 and KuaiRec were built with random-exposure splits in the first place — and why the
benign/harmful split can tell you **how hard to debias a dataset**, but not **which item to
debias**.
